Furnished holiday lets in Le Mans: the rules to know before you rent

By Gardéa·21 November 2026·5 min read

Renting a furnished home to short-stay guests means running a “meublé de tourisme” (furnished holiday let). French law regulates this activity, and the rules have changed a lot in recent years. Here is what to check before you start.

Read this first: this article gives general guidance and does not replace legal or tax advice. The rules change regularly: always check with your local town hall (Le Mans or a nearby town), on service-public.fr and on impots.gouv.fr.
In this article
  1. Declaring to the town hall
  2. Main or second home
  3. Co-ownership and leases
  4. Tax and tourist tax
  5. Insurance

Declaring to the town hall

As a rule, a furnished holiday let must be declared to the town hall of the town where it is located. Depending on the town, this declaration gives you a registration number that must appear on your listing. Ask your town hall about the current procedure.

Main or second home

The rules differ depending on whether it is your main home or another property. Renting out your main home is limited to a certain number of days a year, and towns can set stricter rules. For a second home or an investment property, other formalities may apply.

Co-ownership and leases

  • In a co-owned building: read the co-ownership rules, as some clauses may restrict short-term rentals.
  • If you are a tenant: you need your landlord's written consent to sublet.

Tax and tourist tax

Income from a furnished rental is taxable and must be declared. The tax regime for furnished holiday lets has recently changed: an accountant or impots.gouv.fr can tell you the best option for your situation.

Tourist tax is payable by guests. Depending on the town and the platform, it may be collected directly by the platform: check how it works for your property.

Insurance

Tell your insurer that you rent to guests and check that your policy covers this activity. The cover offered by the platforms has its own conditions: read them carefully.

Getting off to a good start means being compliant first: it protects your property and your income.
